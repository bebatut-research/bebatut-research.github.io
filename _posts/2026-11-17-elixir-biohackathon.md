Each year, the [**ELIXIR BioHackathon**](https://biohackathon-europe.org/) brings researchers, bioinformaticians, and
developers together to build and test tools, share expertise, and spark new
collaborations. The 2026 event took place near Barcelona, Spain, from November 9
to 13, with around 200 participants joining in person and online. Together with
Brieuc Quemeneur (Nantes Université, France) and Nikolaos Strepis (Erasmus MC,
the Netherlands), I co-led the **MiCoKnowGraph** project.

MiCoKnowGraph aims to connect **microbiome data** across domains in a
**collaborative knowledge graph**. Our central question at the hackathon was:
**How can we build such a graph together?**

The team defined **use cases** and **competency questions**, reviewed relevant
**ontologies**, knowledge graphs, and data sources, and explored ways to
harmonize terminology, connect research domains, represent complex observations,
and model the graph.

The hackathon laid the groundwork for continued collaboration, with documented
use cases and curated resources. We are preparing a manuscript for **BioHackrXiv**;
next, we will refine the concepts and relationships and develop and evaluate a
shared graph model.
